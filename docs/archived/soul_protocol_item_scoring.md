# Soul Protocol — Item Scoring System
## Version 0.2 · June 2026 · Solo Developer

> **Purpose:** Defines the point-based scoring system used to assign relative
> values to all items in the game. Scores are used by the Wandering Soul trade
> generation system and Memory Fragment Category A/C scenario design to
> determine fair and unfair trade pairings.
>
> Items are scored on two independent scales — **Durability** and
> **Consumable** — which are not directly comparable. Trades pair like with
> like. Cross-category trades (durability item for consumable) are designer
> decisions made outside the scoring system.
>
> Scores represent **competent play value** — how much an item is worth when
> used at a reasonably optimised time, not at its ceiling and not at its floor.
> All values are pre-playtest placeholders expressing correct relative
> relationships. Absolute values and HP conversion thresholds are expected to
> shift during playtesting.
>
> Confirmed decisions are marked **`✓`**.
> Open questions are marked **`[OPEN]`**.
> Items pending discussion are marked **`[PENDING]`**.

## 1. Scoring Principles

> **✓ Decision: Items are scored compositionally.** Each item's score is the
> sum of its property scores. Properties are scored independently so that
> future items with shared properties inherit consistent values automatically.
> A new item that applies Vulnerable (Physical) twice scores higher than one
> that applies it once without manual re-evaluation of either item.

> **✓ Decision: Scores reflect competent play, not perfect play.** A status
> consumable is scored assuming it is paired with a relevant weapon. A
> branching item is scored as the average of its branches. An elemental item
> is scored assuming a player committed to that element.

> **✓ Decision: Consumables are scored paired with their action.** A
> consumable occupies the Consumable bucket; the player still resolves the
> Action bucket on the same turn. Status consumables are therefore scored
> as the combined value of the status plus the attack that follows it on
> the same turn. The Throw Rock baseline (score 6) is used as the
> conservative action value unless the consumable is explicitly designed
> to pair with a specific weapon.

> **✓ Decision: Branching items average their branches.** Where an item
> produces different effects depending on target state, each branch is scored
> independently and the scores are averaged.

> **✓ Decision: Multi-effect items sum their properties.** An item that
> deals damage and applies a status scores both properties independently
> and sums them.

> **✓ Decision: Healing is scored as damage negated.** Heal values are placed
> on the same scale as damage values. Scoring assumes the player does not
> over-heal — excess healing beyond current HP deficit is not counted.

> **✓ Decision: Timer-2 is the statistical baseline for all status scores.**
> The omen timer distribution is 25% / 50% / 25% for values 1 / 2 / 3.
> Timer-2 is the most probable outcome and is used as the competent baseline
> for all per-tick and shift-triggered status valuations.

> **✓ Decision: Structural and passive items are manually assigned scores.**
> Items whose value is not expressible through damage, charges, scope, or
> status properties receive a manually set score noted with rationale.
> Manual scores are benchmarked against the durability reference point (49)
> to confirm they sit in the expected range.

> **✓ Decision: Cross-category trades are designer decisions.** The scoring
> system does not define a conversion ratio between the Durability and
> Consumable scales. Trades that mix categories (e.g. a durability item
> for a consumable) are authored manually and validated through playtesting
> rather than generated by the formula.

## 2. The Two Scales

All items belong to exactly one scale. Support durability items sit on the
Durability scale because they compete directly with weapons as loot rewards.

| Scale | Item types | Reference point |
|---|---|---|
| **Durability** | Attack (Durability), Support (Durability) | 49 — see below |
| **Consumable** | Consumable | 14 — see below |

### Durability Reference Point — Score 49

An abstract weapon used as the midpoint anchor for the Durability scale.
No real item needs to match this exactly — it is a calibration tool.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Damage per hit | 7 (physical) |
| Scope | Single target |
| Charges | 6 |
| Score | (7 × 2.0 × 1.0) × 3.5 = **49** |

Items scoring well below 49 are weak loot picks relative to a normal drop
weapon. Items scoring well above 49 are strong. Support durability items
are expected to score below 49 — their utility value compensates for lower
raw score. Elite drop weapons are expected to score significantly above 49.

### Consumable Reference Point — Score 14

An abstract consumable used as the midpoint anchor for the Consumable scale.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Status applied | Mid-tier status (base score 7) |
| Paired action | Throw Rock (score 6) |
| Score | 7 + 6 = **14** |

Consumables scoring below 14 are weak relative to the average. Consumables
scoring above 14 are strong. The current consumable range is expected to
run roughly 10–18.

## 3. Property Score Table

The following properties contribute to an item's base score before the
charges multiplier is applied. All values are per-use (one charge).

| Property | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Damage (physical) | 2 per damage unit | Baseline damage type |
| Damage (elemental) | 2.4 per damage unit | 1.2× premium over physical |
| Healing | 2 per HP restored | Same scale as damage; no over-heal |

**Damage unit reference:**

| Source | Damage | Score (1 charge, single target) |
|---|---|---|
| Throw Rock (default strike — always free, never an item) | 3 | 6 |
| Normal drop weapon baseline | 7 | 14 |
| Elite drop weapon baseline | 9 | 18 |

The Throw Rock value (6) represents the floor. Any item that scores below
6 per use provides less value per use than the free default strike and should
be questioned as a design choice.

## 4. Charges Multiplier

Applied to an item's per-use score to produce its total Durability scale
score. The multiplier is dampened — later charges are worth less than earlier
ones to reflect the reliability discount of charges that may never be used
before the item is replaced or the run ends.

| Charges | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0× |
| 2 | 1.7× |
| 3 | 2.3× |
| 4 | 2.8× |
| 5 | 3.2× |
| 6 | 3.5× |
| 7 | 3.8× |
| 8 | 4.1× |
| 9 | 4.4× |
| 10 | 4.7× |
| Each charge beyond 10 | +0.2× |

> **✓ Decision: Charges flatten significantly after 6.** A 10-charge weapon
> is approximately 4.7× a 1-charge weapon, not 10×. This reflects that later
> charges carry increasing uncertainty about whether they will be used.

Consumable items always use the 1.0× multiplier (single use). The charges
multiplier does not apply to the Consumable scale.

---

## 5. Scope Modifier

Modifies the per-use score before the charges multiplier is applied.
Applies to both scales.

| Scope | Modifier | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single target | 1.0× | Baseline |
| AoE (all enemies) | 1.5× | Scored at 2 enemies as competent baseline |

> **✓ Decision: AoE is scored at 1.5× rather than 2×.** Scoring at 2×
> would assume AoE always hits 2 enemies. The discount accounts for single
> enemy encounters where AoE provides no advantage over single target.

## 6. Status Effect Base Scores

Status effects are scored at timer-2 as the statistical baseline. Per-tick
statuses are scored across 2 ticks. Shift-triggered statuses are scored as
the value of one free attack turn enabled or negated at timer-2.

Scores are per-application (one use on one target). When a consumable applies
a status, its score is added to the paired action score to produce the
combined turn value (see Section 7.2).

| Status | Base Score | Trigger | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vulnerable (Physical) | 12 | Passive | Amplifies default damage type; almost always relevant; persists for full cycle |
| Shocked | 10 | Shift | One free attack turn enabled at timer-2; delayed but reliable |
| Exposed | 10 | Shift | Delivers Vulnerable (Physical) after a cycle delay; highly relevant given physical is the default damage type |
| Poisoned | 9 | Per-tick | Escalating DoT; 2 ticks at timer-2 produce strong total damage with no vulnerability dependency |
| Burning | 8 | Per-tick | Flat fire DoT across 2 ticks at timer-2; fire-specific so requires elemental commitment for full combo value |
| Emboldened (Physical) | 8 | Passive | Significant flat bonus to outgoing physical damage; scored for future item use |
| Bleed | 7 | Per-tick | Decaying DoT; timer-2 produces less total damage than Burning due to halving mechanic; physical only |
| Mending | 7 | Per-tick | 2 ticks of healing at timer-2; scored as damage negated |
| Vulnerable (Elemental) | 7 | Passive | Same mechanic as Vulnerable (Physical) but three separate types (Fire / Lightning / Ice) lower average relevance per type |
| Chilled | 6 | Per-tick | Damage reduction not damage dealing; defensive utility with indirect offensive value |
| Hardened | 6 | Per-tick | Per-tick absorption that resets each tick; reliable but ceiling lower than Mending at timer-2 |
| Emboldened (Elemental) | 5 | Passive | Elemental damage multiplier; requires matching element commitment |
| Frenzied | 5 | Passive | Double-edged; risky to apply to player side |
| Type Convert | 4 | Passive | Situational; typically a penalty when it lands on player side |

> **✓ Decision: Vulnerable (Physical) scores highest of all statuses.**
> It amplifies the default damage type, requires no elemental commitment,
> and persists for a full cycle. It is almost always relevant at competent
> play.

> **✓ Decision: Vulnerable (Elemental) scores below Vulnerable (Physical).**
> Three separate elemental types exist. Any given elemental Vulnerable is
> only relevant when the player has committed to that specific element,
> making average relevance lower than physical.

> **✓ Decision: Emboldened (Physical) and Emboldened (Elemental) are
> included in the table as placeholders.** No current items apply these
> statuses directly — they are currently sourced from enemies and the omen
> system. Scores are included for future item design reference.

## 7. Scoring Methods

### 7.1 Damage Item (Attack Durability)

```
Score = (damage_per_hit × damage_type_rate × scope_modifier) × charges_multiplier
```

**Example — Walking Staff (6 physical, single target, 6 charges):**
```
Per-use score = 6 × 2.0 × 1.0 = 12
Total score   = 12 × 3.5 = 42
```

**Example — Rope Flail (4 physical per target, AoE, 6 charges):**
```
Per-use score = 4 × 2.0 × 1.5 = 12
Total score   = 12 × 3.5 = 42
```

### 7.2 Status Consumable

Status consumables are scored as the combined value of the status plus the
paired action on the same turn.

```
Score = status_base_score + paired_action_score
```

Paired action defaults to Throw Rock (6) unless the consumable is explicitly
designed for a specific weapon, in which case that weapon's per-use score
may be substituted.

**Example — Brittle Charm (Vulnerable Physical, single use):**
```
Status score  = 12
Paired action = 6 (Throw Rock baseline)
Total score   = 18
```

**Example — Fire Bomb (Burning, single use):**
```
Status score  = 8
Paired action = 6
Total score   = 14
```

### 7.3 Branching Item

Score each branch independently then average.

**Example — Combustible Oil:**
```
Branch A (target not Burning): Vulnerable (Fire) = 7
Branch B (target Burning):     6 flat fire damage = 6 × 2.4 = 14.4
Average = (7 + 14.4) / 2 = 10.7 → rounded to 11

Add paired action: 11 + 6 = 17
```

### 7.4 Multi-Effect Item

Sum all property scores, then apply charges multiplier if durability.

```
Score = (sum of all property scores × scope_modifier) × charges_multiplier
```

### 7.5 Healing Consumable

```
Score = (HP_restored × 2) + paired_action_score
```

Scored as damage negated plus the action the player takes on the same turn.

### 7.6 Support Durability Item

Support durability items are scored using the manual method where the effect
is not expressible as damage or status. The score is benchmarked against the
durability reference point (49) and the rationale is recorded per item.

For support items with a scorable status effect, the status base score is
used as the per-use score, then the charges multiplier is applied:

```
Score = status_base_score × charges_multiplier
```

### 7.7 Structural / Passive Item (Manual)

Score is manually assigned. The assigned score and rationale are recorded
in the Current Item Scores section. Manual scores are benchmarked against
the relevant scale reference point.

## 8. HP Conversion

Items can be traded for HP (or HP for items) in Wandering Soul encounters.
The following conversion buckets translate item scores into HP equivalents.
Two separate bucket tables exist — one per scale.

HP amounts are marked `[OPEN]` — values are set once vessel HP pools and
average incoming damage per encounter are established through playtesting.
Bucket thresholds may also shift once real item scores are validated.

### Durability Scale — HP Conversion

| Durability Score | HP Trade Value |
|---|---|
| 1–25 | Low HP `[OPEN]` |
| 26–49 | Medium HP `[OPEN]` |
| 50–75 | High HP `[OPEN]` |
| 76–100 | Very High HP `[OPEN]` |
| 101+ | Maximum HP `[OPEN]` |

### Consumable Scale — HP Conversion

| Consumable Score | HP Trade Value |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | Low HP `[OPEN]` |
| 11–14 | Medium HP `[OPEN]` |
| 15–18 | High HP `[OPEN]` |
| 19+ | Very High HP `[OPEN]` |

> **✓ Decision: HP conversion is vessel-agnostic at MVP.** All vessels use
> the same conversion tables. Per-vessel HP tuning may be introduced
> post-MVP if playtesting reveals that HP trades feel significantly different
> in value between vessels.

## 9. Trade Fairness Tolerance

### Same-Category Trades

Trades pair items of the same scale. The fairness tolerance applies
independently within each scale.

> **✓ Decision: Fair trade tolerance is ±20% of the higher-scored item.**
> Two items scoring 40 and 49 are fair (gap is 18% of 49).
> Two items scoring 20 and 49 are not fair (gap is 59% of 49).

### Category A Memory Fragment Scenarios

Category A presents a fair trade with a walk-away option. Both sides of the
trade must fall within the ±20% tolerance window. Trades mixing scales
(durability for consumable) are permitted as designer decisions but must
be manually validated rather than formula-generated.

### Category C Memory Fragment Scenarios

Category C presents two options both of which cost something. The unfairness
of Option 1 must be quantifiable:

> **✓ Decision: Category C unfair trades target a score gap of at least
> 30% above the fair tolerance window.** If fair tolerance is ±20%, an
> unfair trade should present Option 1 at roughly 50%+ above the value
> of what the player receives. Option 2's cost must always be lower than
> Option 1's price — the player should feel the unfairness while recognising
> that cutting losses is a real alternative.

### Cross-Category Trades

> **✓ Decision: Cross-category trades (durability for consumable) are
> designer decisions made outside the scoring formula.** They are permitted
> in Wandering Soul encounters and Memory Fragment scenarios but must be
> manually authored and playtested rather than generated by the scoring
> system. No conversion ratio between the two scales is defined.

### Known Issues for Playtesting Review

**Durability scale — elite weapons clustering high (89–108)**
Soldier's Blade, Smoldering Brand, Glacial Brand, and Arc Wand all score
well above the reference point. This may be correct for elite drops, or may
indicate the charges multiplier flattens too slowly at high charge counts.
Arc Wand at 108 is the most extreme — the arc scoring method may be
overvaluing the secondary hit.

**Battered Sword on normal drop pool (62)**
Scores notably above other normal drop weapons due to charge count.
May indicate Battered Sword charges are too high relative to its damage,
or that the charges multiplier penalises burst weapons (Cracked Cudgel at 41)
too harshly.

**Lucky Paw (18)**
Manual score may be too low. Passive physical evasion per combat has
compounding run value that is difficult to express in a single score.
Revisit once evasion percentage is confirmed.

**Frost Shard (12) as elite drop**
Scores below the consumable reference point despite being an elite drop.
Chilled's base score (6) may need to be revisited, or Frost Shard may
need an additional effect to justify elite placement.

**Elemental weapons (39) vs physical weapons (41–42) on normal drops**
The gap between elemental and physical normal drop weapons is small —
about 5%. Elemental weapons have fewer charges which suppresses their
score despite the damage type premium. This may be correct given the
commitment cost of elemental builds, or may make elemental weapons feel
like a poor loot pick. Worth watching in playtesting.
